## Script

### Part 1 — The scenario and the user *(0:00 – 0:45)*

*(Slide: title + the user)*

> Have you ever opened a job website and thought: "So many jobs — but which one is actually
> right for me?"
>
> That's our starting point. A job website shows you **what jobs exist**. It never tells you
> **which job is worth going after** — and that question decides your next few years.
>
> **Our user is a job seeker or career switcher in Singapore** — someone standing at a fork,
> deciding which career track to aim at next. Our secondary user is the career coach
> advising them.

### Part 2 — The objective *(0:45 – 1:30)*

*(Slide: the four dimensions)*

> **The decision we help with is: which career track should I target next?**
>
> That decision depends on four things, and no job website shows them side by side:
>
> - **Demand** — are there enough openings to realistically land one?
> - **Pay** — how much does it pay, at my level?
> - **Competition** — how many people want the same seat?
> - **Accessibility** — can I get in with the experience I have today?
>
> So we built **Career Compass SG**: a dashboard that scores **215 career tracks** — 43 job
> categories across 5 seniority levels — on all four, using **1,044,597 real Singapore job
> postings** from October 2022 to May 2024.

### Part 3 — Success criteria *(1:30 – 2:00)*

> How do we know if it works? We gave ourselves a simple test: **the three-minute test.**
>
> A user opens the dashboard, and within three minutes they can say: *"these are the three
> tracks I should target, this is what they pay, and this is how crowded they are."*
> Everything in the product exists to pass that test.

### Part 4 — Handover *(2:00 – 2:15)*

> One honest note: this was **not our first idea**. We started with a simple "top-paying
> jobs" ranking. The data itself killed that idea — pay, demand and competition turned out
> to be almost unrelated, so ranking on any one of them is misleading.
>
> **So that's the question we set out to answer. Now — how we turned one million messy rows
> into data we could trust.**

## Questions you own

**"Why not just use LinkedIn or MyCareersFuture?"**
> Those sites show job posts. They never show how many applicants compete for each seat, and
> they never compare career tracks against each other. That comparison is our whole product.

**"Who exactly would pay for / use this?"**
> Job seekers and switchers directly; career coaches as a client tool. Today it runs
> locally; hosting it as a public site is the natural next step, and the analytics already
> handle the full million rows.

**"Can a dashboard really answer 'which career should I pick'?"**
> Not alone — and we say so inside the app. Our score ranks *market conditions*; whether you
> would enjoy the work is your half of the decision.
